The dance floor: how many more panels?

    Built for a laptop or a projector. On a phone, turn it sideways for a bigger picture.

    For teachers: what this page is

    One idea, shown

    A prototype of a different way to build a MathLab tool. It covers one idea: a squared binomial is a real square made of four pieces, (x + a)² = x² + 2ax + a². It is meant to sit beside your lesson, before or after it. It does not replace the lesson.

    What students do

    • Guess, and see the guess on the floor. An order of 9 covers only the corner.
    • Count it their own way. They color the gap in chunks and the page writes their number sentence. Then they see four other colorings of the same gap. It is a version of the well-known border problem, set on a dance floor.
    • Move the picture, watch the equation. They drag x and a; every term in the formula is a piece they can tap.
    • Build it backward. They drag six strips onto a square, find the hole, and complete the square.
    • One dimension up. They pull apart (x + a)³ and count 1, 3, 3, 1.
    • Tell the story and copy it into Canvas.

    Ways to use it

    • Projected: the chapter buttons jump anywhere. The "In class" lines are short prompts for the room.
    • On laptops: about 12 minutes, alone or in pairs.
    • Homework or catching up: students paste their story into a Canvas text entry.

    What it leaves out

    Unequal growth, (x + a)(x + b), negative terms, and factoring trinomials that are not perfect squares. The floor is counted in panels, so there are no prices or measurements to check. The current tool covers those: rjedtech.github.io/Raider-MathLab/#areamodel.
